# Currency and Control

### China's Dedollarisation Efforts within the Petrodollar System

This repository contains my undergraduate dissertation completed as part of my BA in Politics, Philosophy and Economics (PPE) at the University of Sheffield.

The dissertation examines whether China's efforts to reduce reliance on the US dollar represent a genuine challenge to the petrodollar system or a strategic response to a global monetary order that remains overwhelmingly dollar-centric.

---

## Research Question

> Are we witnessing a shift in global monetary power, or is the resilience of the US dollar being underestimated?

---

## Overview

In recent years, dedollarisation has become an increasingly prominent topic in international political economy. China's attempts to expand the role of the Renminbi (RMB) in trade, finance, and reserve holdings have prompted debate over whether the global monetary system is becoming less dependent on the US dollar.

This dissertation investigates these claims through an empirical analysis of key economic indicators associated with China's dedollarisation strategy and the wider petrodollar system.

---

## Data

The study focuses on four variables:

- China's Foreign Exchange Reserves
- USD/CNY Exchange Rate
- Brent Crude Oil Prices
- RMB Internationalisation (SWIFT RMB Tracker)

---

## Methodology

The analysis employs a range of time-series econometric techniques, including:

- Bai-Perron Structural Break Analysis
- Vector Autoregression (VAR)
- Granger Causality Testing
- ARIMA Modelling and Forecasting

---

## Key Findings

- China's dedollarisation efforts appear largely reactive rather than linear.
- Significant changes coincide with major geopolitical and economic events.
- Oil market dynamics continue to influence RMB exchange-rate behaviour.
- Despite increasing internationalisation of the RMB, the US dollar remains deeply embedded within the global financial and energy systems.
